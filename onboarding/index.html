Set up with Claude

This is a one-off, about 15-20 minutes. You'll need Claude Code installed and signed in with your Claude Pro account (search "Claude Code install" on Anthropic's site; it runs in your terminal).

  1. Open a terminal in this jobscan folder and start Claude Code: claude
  2. Type: /setup Claude reads your answers and your CV, asks a few follow-up questions, then writes three things you can check and edit:
    • me/profile.md - who you are and what you want, written so Claude can judge jobs consistently.
    • me/master.json - your CV as structured data, taken only from what your CV says.
    • config.yaml - your locations, filters, and the companies to watch, each one tested live.
  3. Read me/profile.md carefully and correct anything that isn't you. It's the single thing that decides which jobs you see.

Your daily routine

Two steps, a few minutes a day. Employers rarely post at weekends, so weekdays are enough.

  1. Collect new jobs (free - no Claude involved): .venv/bin/python -m jobscan collect It checks every company on your list (on Windows: .venv\Scripts\python -m jobscan collect) and tells you how many new jobs passed your filters. Double-click collect.command (Mac) or collect.bat (Windows) if you prefer.
  2. Review them in Claude Code: /review-jobs Claude reads each new job against your profile, gives it a score out of 10 and a one-line reason, and rebuilds your dashboard. Strong matches are listed at the end.
  3. Open dashboard.html (or your dashboard while this page is running) to see everything scored, best first.

Your Pro plan has usage limits that reset every few hours. A normal day's batch is fine; if a first review has hundreds of jobs, Claude will work through them in chunks and tell you where it stopped.

Adding companies

The easy way

In Claude Code, type:

/add-company Spotify

Claude finds where the company posts jobs, works out which job platform it uses, tests it live, checks it's really that company (and not another firm with a similar name), and only then adds it. If the company uses a platform jobscan can't read yet, Claude says so instead of guessing.

By hand

Every company lives in config.yaml under the platform it uses. Test before adding:

.venv/bin/python -m jobscan employer-test greenhouse '{"name": "Monzo", "board": "monzo"}' .venv/bin/python -m jobscan employer-add greenhouse '{"name": "Monzo", "board": "monzo"}'

On Windows, use .venv\Scripts\python in place of .venv/bin/python.

If a test returns 0 jobs, the details are wrong - don't add it. A company with a broken entry is just skipped, but it will never show you anything.

How adapters work

Companies don't build their own job sites; they rent one from a handful of platforms - Workday, Greenhouse, Ashby and so on. Each platform publishes its jobs in a predictable way. An adapter is the small piece of code that knows how to read one platform, so one adapter covers every company that uses it.

Your profile and CV

Two files make jobscan yours. /setup writes first drafts of both; you own them after that.

me/profile.md - how jobs are judged

Claude reads this before every review. The best profiles are specific about why, not just what:

  • The one-line version: what you're trying to do with your career right now.
  • Target roles, in tiers: what's a strong fit, a good fit, and a maybe.
  • What you're moving away from: and how to recognise it in a job description, even when the title looks right.
  • Level rules: titles that are too senior or too junior for you.
  • Worked examples: a few real postings with the score you'd give them and why. These do more than any rule - add one whenever you disagree with a score.

me/master.json - your CV, as data

Every application is written from this file and nothing else. That's the safety rule: Claude may reorder, emphasise and reword what's in it, but never add a skill, number or achievement that isn't there. If something true is missing from your CV, add it here first.

Make it yours, not a copy of someone else's

  • Use your own words in profile.md. If a line sounds like a job advert, Claude can't tell what you actually want.
  • Keep master.json complete and factual: dates, numbers, tools. Tailoring happens per application, not here.
  • Re-run /setup whenever your direction changes.

Writing an application

When a job is worth applying for, in Claude Code type:

/write-pack

and name the job (or paste its key from the dashboard). Claude writes a tailored CV and cover letter from me/master.json, checks every claim against it, and saves both as PDFs in applications/. Read both before you send them - you're the final check.

PDFs need Google Chrome or Chromium installed; jobscan uses it to print them with real, searchable text.

Questions

Does this cost anything?

No, beyond the Claude Pro plan you already have. Collecting jobs is free; the judging happens in your own Claude Code session.

Is my data sent anywhere?

Your answers and CV stay in the me/ folder on this laptop. When you run Claude Code, the files Claude reads are sent to Claude to be processed, as with anything else you share with it.

A company shows 0 jobs.

Either it has no openings in your area, or its job site changed. Ask Claude: "check why <company> returns nothing" - it will re-test it.

I disagree with a score.

Tell Claude why, then ask it to add that job to the worked examples in me/profile.md. That's how the scoring learns your taste.

How do I stop this page?

Close the browser tab and press Ctrl+C in the terminal window that opened it. Start it again with start.command, start.sh or start.bat.
